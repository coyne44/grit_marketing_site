If you run a cash-based or membership chiropractic practice, most EHRs are selling you a solution to a problem you don't have. They're built around insurance billing — claim submission, ERA posting, clearinghouse fees, eligibility checks — and they price accordingly. You end up paying for a billing department you'll never staff.

Here's how to think about software when insurance isn't the center of your practice.

## What a cash practice actually needs

Strip it down and the daily job of running a cash-forward practice is short:

- **Scheduling** that's fast to glance at and easy to rebook from.
- **Clinical notes** built for chiropractic — SOAP notes you can chart in under a minute, not a generic medical template you fight with.
- **Payments** that just work: take a card, run a membership or visit package, hand over a receipt.
- **A patient portal** so people can book, check in, fill out intake, and message you without calling.
- **Reminders** by text so nobody no-shows.
- **Superbills** for the patients who self-submit to insurance — which is the *patient's* job in a cash practice, not yours.

That's the whole list for most solo cash practices. Notice what's not on it.

## What you're probably overpaying for

Insurance-first EHRs bundle in — and charge for — a stack of features a cash practice rarely uses:

- **Claim submission (EDI 837)** and clearinghouse connections
- **ERA/EOB auto-posting**
- **Eligibility and benefits verification**
- **Multi-provider and multi-location management**
- **Complex coding and modifier tooling**

If you bill insurance heavily, this is the engine of your business. If you don't, it's dead weight you're financing every month. Vendors like ChiroFusion build their entire model on the assumption that insurance billing is essential — which is exactly the wrong assumption for a cash practice.

## "But what about the patients who use insurance?"

In a cash practice, the usual answer is a **superbill**: an itemized receipt the patient submits to their own insurer for possible reimbursement. You generate it; they file it. You stay out of the claims business entirely. Any software you choose should produce superbills cleanly. You don't need a clearinghouse to do that.

## How to evaluate a cash-practice EHR

A few questions that quickly separate the right fit from the wrong one:

1. **Is it chiropractic-specific, or general wellness?** Generic tools (built for massage, physio, mental health) tend to make the spinal/clinical workflow an afterthought. You'll feel it every single note.
2. **What's the *all-in* monthly price?** Add up the base plan, SMS fees, payment fees, and per-provider charges. Compare totals, not sticker prices.
3. **Is there a contract or setup fee?** Month-to-month with no setup fee means the software has to keep earning your business.
4. **How fast can you actually chart?** Sit down with the trial and write three notes. Speed here is the difference between leaving on time and charting at 7pm.
5. **Is HIPAA handled — with a BAA included?** This is non-negotiable, and it shouldn't cost extra.
